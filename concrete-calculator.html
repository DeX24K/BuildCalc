Concrete Calculator

Get the exact cubic yards and number of 60 or 80 lb bags of concrete you need for slabs, footings, columns and stairs.

Project size

How it works: cubic feet = length × width × (thickness ÷ 12). Divide by 27 for cubic yards. A 80 lb bag = 0.60 cu ft, a 60 lb bag = 0.45 cu ft.

How many bags of concrete do I need?

Calculate the volume of your slab in cubic feet (length × width × thickness in feet), divide by 27 to get yards, then divide by the bag size. A standard 10×10 slab, 4" thick, needs about 1.23 cubic yards — roughly 56 bags of 80 lb mix.

Tip: for anything larger than a 2-yard pour, order a ready-mix truck. It's cheaper and the finish is more consistent than bagging it yourself.

Frequently asked questions

How much does a cubic yard of concrete weigh?

About 4,050 lb. One 80 lb bag = 0.6 cubic feet, so ~45 bags make one cubic yard.

Should I add extra concrete?

Yes — add 5–10% for spillage, uneven subgrade and rounding. It's cheaper to have a little extra than to run short mid-pour.
